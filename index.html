A toy model of the step before biology

Abiogenesis

How could life begin from matter that is not alive? Below, water, methane and amino acid chains drift in a primordial soup. Touch it.

Move to pull molecules in. Tap to drop a chain (a ripple spreads when Effects are on). Press and hold to stir a current. Hover a chain to read its letters. Warmer means faster Brownian motion; acid or base (away from pH 7) makes chains degrade sooner and tints the glow.

This is a live, animated simulation drawn on a canvas that screen readers skip. In words: water and methane molecules drift with Brownian motion. Chains of amino acids, written as letters, appear at random, mutate by substitution, insertion or deletion, recombine when two meet, and eventually degrade. The readout and the lab notebook below describe what is happening, and the controls change the temperature and pH.

    What it simulates, from soup to cells

    The pictures below are small in-page illustrations of how each feature works in the app, not screenshots.

    1. Feature 1

      Basic simulation

      Molecules, movement, collisions, temperature and pH, amino acid chain generation, mutation and recombination, pattern tracking, interactive tooltips and a launcher. This is the soup above.

    2. Feature 2

      Environment

      Temperature, pH and wetness that cycle over time (wet and dry pools, day and night), with controls for both. As the pool dries, chains crowd together, and where they do a protocell forms: a droplet that keeps its chains in and makes them mutate faster. It bursts when the pool refills.

    3. Feature 3

      Chemistry and emergence

      Lipids and nucleotides. Nucleotides join into RNA-like strands, strands copy themselves into their complement (with the odd mistake) when there are building blocks nearby, and a strand carrying a catalytic motif copies four times as fast. Every molecule has a fitness, and fit strands last longer, so the soup fills with strands that are better at surviving and copying.

    4. Feature 4

      Visualization and metrics

      Proteins read off RNA strands by the genetic code: a strand that holds the start codon AUG carries a gene, and now and then it makes its protein as a new amino acid chain. A Genes and Proteins screen plays the whole story as an animated scene (nucleotides forming RNA or DNA, copying, then translation by a ribosome). Live charts track diversity, strands and the environment, and a protein lineage shows who came from whom.

      Charts fill in as the soup runs.

    5. Feature 5

      Cells to complex life

      From droplets to organisms. Cells form from lipid bilayers around strands, then grow and divide, and fitter cells divide more. Eukaryotes follow: one cell engulfs another, which becomes the mitochondrion, and the strands move into their own envelope, the nucleus. Cells that stay together form colonies whose members take roles (germ, soma and feeder cells).

    How it works: every chain keeps a diary

    In the app, every chain has an id. Each mutation and each recombination is written to an event log together with its parents, so any chain's ancestry can be rebuilt afterwards. The tree below is drawn from the log of the live soup above, right now.

    20 letters

    Chains are made from the 20 amino acids, written by letter code. Colour shows the chemistry class.

    ARNDCQEGHILKMFPSTWYV

    hydrophobic polar basic acidic

    Mutation

    A chain may replace one residue, insert a new one, or delete one. The child replaces its parent but gets a new id linked to it. Chains stay between 1 and 50 residues.

    Recombination

    When two chains meet they may swap pieces: the head of one up to a random cut, joined to the tail of the other from a random cut. The child is new, the parents stay.

    Chance and METHINKS

    The app watches for the sequence METHINKS (8 letters, from Hamlet). By pure luck that is about 1 in 25.6 billion per try, so you will almost never see it emerge here. That gap is why selection matters. The button in the tray plants it by hand, and says so in the notebook.

    Lineage tree

    Pick a chain that is alive now. Its ancestors grow to the left, newest to the right. Magenta dashed lines are recombinations. Click a node to re-root the tree there.

      macOS

      The app is not notarized. The first time, right-click (or Control-click) Abiogenesis in Applications, choose Open, then confirm Open. Or run once in Terminal: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Abiogenesis.app

      Linux and plain binaries
